# Localization — principally DACH / German exec structure and tone

This is not translation. It is restructuring and re-toning so the copy lands for the target
market — most often a DACH (Germany/Austria/Switzerland) executive audience, and it applies
**even when the copy stays in English**, because a German reader carries German business-prose
expectations into an English document.

Anglo-American consulting prose reads as glib, salesy, or unserious to a German exec. The move
is toward structure, substance, and directness.

---

## What DACH exec readers expect

- **Structure up front, explicit logic.** State the WHY, then WHAT, then HOW — in that order,
  named as such if it helps. German business writing rewards a visible, front-loaded argument
  skeleton over a narrative that unfolds.
- **Substance over rhetoric.** Claims are backed or they are cut. A vivid line with nothing
  under it reads as an attempt to distract, not to persuade.
- **Every number sourced.** A figure without a source is a liability, not a highlight.
  Attribute it or drop it. Never present a hoped-for client delta as if it were measured.
- **Directness over enthusiasm.** No manufactured punchlines, no applause-line closers, no
  "imagine a world where…", no exclamation. Confidence is shown by precision, not volume.
- **Formality and precision.** Formal register (the equivalent of *Sie*). Precise terms,
  consistently used — inventing synonyms for the same concept reads as imprecision, not
  variety (this pairs with the terminology ledger in `positioning`).

## Moves that translate the tone

| Anglo-marketing move | DACH-appropriate replacement |
|---|---|
| "Unlock the power of…" | State what it does and what that yields. |
| Punchy fragment for effect ("Faster. Safer. Simpler.") | One full sentence with the actual claim and its basis. |
| Rhetorical question as a hook | A stated proposition. |
| "This is a game-changer." | The specific, sourced change and its magnitude. |
| A closing flourish | Stop at the last substantive point, or state the decision asked for. |

## Structural localization: WHY → WHAT → HOW

A house structure that lands for DACH exec audiences (observed in an early-stage
operating-model house deck a practice adopted as its standard): **WHY** (the situation and what forces a decision) →
**WHAT** (the target state / capability, described concretely) → **HOW** (the path, phased) →
optionally **WHO / WHEN / Next steps**. Pair with MBB discipline: action-title headings, one
message per section, source every number, no decoration, and the 60-second rule (an exec
should get the message of a section in under a minute). This complements `storyline`'s
Minto/SCQA — SCQA earns the governing thought; WHY/WHAT/HOW is the section spine beneath it.

## Guardrails

- Localize structure and tone; **do not** invent claims to fill the "substance" bar. If the
  substance isn't there, that's a `positioning`/proof-sourcing gap, not a writing fix.
- If output must be in German, flag that native review is needed — this method fixes structure
  and tone, not native-speaker idiom.
- Confirm the target market with the user. These are DACH norms; other markets differ (and
  some of these moves would read as *cold* to a US SMB audience). Don't apply by reflex.
