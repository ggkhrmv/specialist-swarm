# The standing author profile

The default author voice for this installation. Read it whenever the asset carries their name, and
keep it current: every new set of rewrites sharpens it (see `voice-matching.md` §1b).

Derived from **19 of the author's own rewrites of generated copy** across two rounds. Rules 9–16 only
became visible in the second round, when they rewrote *whole passages* rather than single sentences —
which is why passage-scale samples are worth asking for.

For a different author, build a fresh profile in the engagement's voice bar. Do not apply this one to
someone else's asset.

---

## The dominant move: compress, then state the mechanism

Across the second-round rewrites the author cut **40–65% of the words while keeping every load-bearing
fact**. They do not hedge or soften their way to brevity — they delete the scaffolding and state the
mechanism. Seven sentences became four; four became one; five became three.

If your draft is not materially shorter than your instinct, it is not in this voice yet.

---

## Rules

**1. One umbrella actor carries the through-line.** Pick the canonical umbrella term for the subject
and use it consistently, rather than alternating between a specific name, a generic noun, and "it".
Named parts sit *beneath* the umbrella term, not alongside it.

**2. Name the audience in their own terms** — the people who use and operate the thing, not
organisational abstractions.

**3. Vision verbs up front, first person plural.** "We envision…", "We want to build…", "The goal
is…".

**4. Short declarative sentences. More full stops, few subordinate clauses.** Break long
comma-or-dash chains into two or three crisp sentences. **No connective em dashes** (a locked ban).

**5. Present tense, factual, low hedge.** "The platform already offers self-service, but it is not
widely adopted."

**6. Recurring qualifiers** they actually use: "under human governance", "autonomously", "compliant".

**7. Flat cause→effect chains.** "The demand is high, so the work overspills, and the team gets
pulled into operational work."

**8. State what a thing IS or OWNS, not what it lacks.** "holds no knowledge of its own" → "only owns
the conversation memory". Prefer the positive-ownership frame over the absence frame.

**9. Never narrate the artefact.** A hard ban on self-referential signposting: no "The diagram shows
how", "The table below shows…", "This picture picks up at…". The diagram or table speaks for itself.
If a table genuinely needs a lead-in, a six-word colon pointer is acceptable — "Each gate, and where
it stands today:".

**10. Reorder to end on the consequence.** Status first, effort-implication last. "Building those
gates is most of the work. Most are still on paper." → "Most of those gates are still on paper.
Building them is most of the work."

**11. Kill the storytelling register.** No narrative scene-setting. Label the thing and state it:
"One possible use case shows…", not "A user hits a wall the platform cannot yet clear."

**12. Drop rhetorical set-ups and self-praise about rigour.** Cut "This is the case to invest, and we
make it with our own numbers", "On our own conservative grading", "Each team already owns a proven
piece". State the finding and let it carry itself.

**13. "Opportunity to invest and define" over "the case to invest."** Forward-looking and
participative, not persuasive.

**14. Passive is fine when the actor is obvious or irrelevant.** "Customer-facing capability is built
after…", "The specific technologies will be settled in the design phase". Do **not** force these into
active voice.

**15. The author's own plain intensifiers stay.** They will write "the true power of X" where a
de-slop pass would strike it. **Do not remove an intensifier the author introduced** — flag it and
let them rule.

## Hard bans (locked)

**flywheel · bet(s) · de-risk(ed/ing) · compound(ing) as a verb · substrate · unlock / "value
unlocked" · leverage · seamless · robust · harness · empower · streamline · "pays its own way" ·
triadic slogans · em-dash-as-connective in exec copy · X-becomes-Y transformation cadence.**

Exec register holds at **zero** visible watchlist hits. The technical/architect register keeps its
precise engineering vocabulary — do not flatten it.

## Grammar slips to fix silently

When the author writes in a second language and has asked for grammar to be fixed silently, fix these
without flagging, and preserve rhythm and stance:

- Comma before "that" in a reported clause: "shows, that no capability…" → no comma.
- Singular/plural agreement after "and": "the mechanisms and foundation **has** been verified" →
  "have been".
- Missing article before a named actor: "Intake Agent can be built" → "**The** Intake Agent".
- "use-case" as a noun → "use case" (hyphenate only when attributive).
- Verb form after "and": "connects planned work and **build** the boundaries" → "**builds**".
- Dropped "the" before a plural of specifics: "picks up structured requests" → "picks up **the**
  structured requests" where a specific set is meant.
- "how it should look **like**" → "how it should look" (or "what it should look like").
- Article drops ("into platform's" → "the platform's"), possessive apostrophes ("operator's" →
  "operators'"), "setup" → "set up", "overtime" → "over time", restrictive-clause commas.

List any change you made **beyond their wording** so they can rule on it. Fixing a slip is silent;
changing a choice is not.

## Register and market

- **Register map:** exec view = zero AI tells, claim plus consequence. Technical view = keep the
  precise engineering register.
- **Market:** DACH / German exec audience. WHY→WHAT→HOW spine; source every number and grade. See
  `localization.md`.
- **Standing requirement:** the asset must not read as AI-generated, and must not *look*
  AI-generated. Run the full pass — this profile, `humanizer`, and the machine tell-scan — at Build
  and at Review.
