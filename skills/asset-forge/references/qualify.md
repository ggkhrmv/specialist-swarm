# Qualify — bid/no-bid checklist

Run this at the end of Intake. The output is an explicit go or no-go, not a soft "probably worth pursuing." Present the assessment to the user, then get a decision. Record both the decision and the rationale in `asset-forge-state.md`.

---

## Standard qualify (competitive tender)

Work through each factor. Characterize each as green, amber, or red before summarizing.

### Win probability

- **Relationship.** Do you have a connection to the economic buyer, or are you coming in cold? Is there a coach inside the client who actively wants you to win?
- **Scope fit.** Does the requirement set match your genuine strengths, or does it favor a competitor's capabilities? Can you evidence your claims with named references the client will recognize?
- **Competition.** Who else is bidding? Is there an incumbent defending this contract — and if so, what is your differentiated reason for the client to switch? Is there a competitor who is already trusted by the client in this domain?
- **Brief maturity.** Is the requirement set well-formed, or are key requirements fuzzy or contradictory? Immature briefs can favor the partner who helps frame the solution — or they can signal a client who does not yet know what they want.

### Walk-away criteria

Stop and recommend no-go if any of these apply:

- The submission timeline is impossible to meet without cutting corners on quality or honesty.
- The client has a preferred winner and is running a compliance exercise. Evidence: they are unwilling to extend deadlines, the requirements mirror a specific competitor's offering, or a coach has said so directly.
- The scope requires claiming capabilities you do not have and cannot evidence with real references.
- The commercial model demanded (pricing structure, liability terms, SLA thresholds) would make the engagement financially unsafe or delivery-dangerous.
- There is a conflict of interest you cannot disclose or cannot manage within the submission.

### Recommendation

After working through the factors: state the win probability (high / medium / low), state whether any walk-away criterion is triggered, and give a recommendation. Present this to the user and get an explicit go / no-go before moving to Frame.

If the user overrides a no-go recommendation: record the override and the reasoning in the state file.

## Lightweight qualify (non-competitive assets)

For internal POVs, readouts, briefings, and any asset where there is no external competition, a formal bid/no-bid decision is not required. But three questions still need answers before building:

1. **Is the purpose clear?** What is this asset trying to do, and for whom? If the purpose is vague, clarify before building — a POV without a thesis is a document without a reason to exist.
2. **Is there a real decision or action?** What does the audience need to decide or do after reading this? If the answer is "nothing specific," ask what success looks like before proceeding.
3. **Is there enough material?** Does sufficient data, analysis, or evidence exist to support the argument you intend to make? If not, what needs to be gathered first — and who is responsible for gathering it?

If any of these three is unclear, resolve it before moving to Frame. A few minutes spent here prevents a rebuild later.
