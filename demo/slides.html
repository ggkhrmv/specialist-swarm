How it works · Claude Managed Agents, one session

Playbooks are Skills: plain markdown in skills/, owned by each team2 / 5
We built a deal desk on Claude Managed Agents. A Senior Partner agent reads the RFP and briefs four specialists at once. Each has its own context and judges the RFP against the firm's rulebook for its lane: the pricing playbook, the legal checklist, the capability map, the battlecards. Those rulebooks are plain markdown files the business owns. Then the partner reads the four reports against each other and writes the proposal.

Why the cross-check matters · trap C5

Each report is right alone. Together they reveal that the exit cancels the discount

Acme asked for 35% off and a free exit. Read one report at a time, both look acceptable. The partner tied the bigger discount to the commitment that justifies it, and let Acme choose.

From rehearsal run 2's proposal-response.docx, commercial section4 / 5
This is why the cross-check matters. Pricing says 25% off is the ceiling, and only with a three-year commitment. Legal says an exit at will is negotiable. Each report is fine on its own. Read together, a 30-day exit cancels the commitment the discount pays for. The partner caught that: 20% off with flexibility, or 25% only for a committed three-year term. Acme chooses.
