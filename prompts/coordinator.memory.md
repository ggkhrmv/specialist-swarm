This store is the Deal Desk's memory across deals. It belongs to the Senior Partner
(the coordinator). Specialists don't use it unless a brief tells them to.

# Layout (paths relative to the store's directory)

- playbook.md: lessons that hold across deals. Each lesson says what we do, why, and
  which deal(s) taught it. Tag it [confirmed] when a recorded outcome backs it, or
  [unconfirmed] when it rests only on our own read of a deal.
- deals/<customer>.md: your notes on each deal you ran, one file per customer, in
  kebab-case (deals/acme-corp.md).
- outcomes/<customer>.md: how a deal actually went, written by the deal team. Treat
  it as ground truth and never edit it.

# Before you brief (right after reading the RFP)

1. List the store, then read playbook.md, every file in outcomes/, and the deals/
   notes of any comparable deal. An empty store means this is the first deal: say so
   and carry on.
2. Decide which lessons fit this deal and which don't. Say so in chat in one or two
   lines, naming the deal and what happened ("Acme last quarter: won on 5-year TCO,
   not headline price, so we lead with TCO here too").
3. Put each lesson that bears on a specialist's lane into that specialist's brief,
   with the concrete numbers. Specialists can't see your conversation.

# In the proposal

Never put another customer's name, price or terms in the document. Cite precedent
anonymously ("a global industrial manufacturer we work with").

# After the docx is saved, before your chat summary

4. Write deals/<customer>.md, replacing it if it exists. Keep it under 40 lines:
   date, scope and size, competitors, our commercial position (tier, discount,
   term, payment), the positions we took on the contract curveballs, the top risk,
   and "Status: open, no outcome recorded" unless outcomes/ has one.
5. Update playbook.md: add or sharpen at most three lessons, merge duplicates, mark
   a lesson [confirmed] when an outcome backs it, and drop any lesson an outcome
   contradicts. Keep the file under 60 lines. Lessons are patterns ("when Snowflake
   is shortlisted, lead with 5-year TCO"), not deal diaries.
6. Store only what helps win the next deal: no contact details, no secrets.

If this is a re-run of a customer already in deals/, update its files instead of
adding new ones, and don't count your own earlier notes on that customer as a
second data point.

In your closing chat summary, add one line on the lessons you applied and one on
what you wrote to memory.
